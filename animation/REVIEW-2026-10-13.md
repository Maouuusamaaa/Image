# Animation Project Review — 2026-10-13

## Scope and evidence
Reviewed the local `Stickman-Battle-Animation-Project.zip` source package, including `scripts/render_battle.py`, `tests/test_renderer.py`, project notes, and the bundled MP4 metadata/content presence. The GitHub repository also contains `animation/Stickman-Battle-Animation-Revision-v8.zip`. These are separate artifacts; their contents have not been proven byte-identical. This review does not claim a frame-by-frame visual approval of the GitHub ZIP.

## Verification performed
- Python unit tests: **4 passed**.
- Test coverage currently verifies frame count, dimensions/color mode, time variation, and phase-helper bounds.
- Renderer is deterministic in intent and uses Pillow for drawing and FFmpeg for H.264 encoding.

## Findings

### High priority — visual correctness
1. **Contact effects are time-triggered, not contact-triggered.** Effects and camera shake use fixed timestamps (7.4, 11.8, 17.5, 21.2, 24.4 seconds). The renderer does not calculate whether a fist overlaps the opponent's head or torso before showing an impact. This can produce a hit cue without visible contact.
2. **Hand positions do not use a joint solver.** Arm endpoints are calculated from the same direction coefficients at fixed multipliers. Because the direction vector is not normalized and the elbow/hand offsets are applied independently, effective limb lengths change with pose values. This contributes to inconsistent anatomy and odd hand placement.
3. **Foot planting is not constrained.** Foot x/y coordinates are recomputed from each frame's pose and stride. There is no planted-foot lock or inverse-kinematics correction, so support feet can slide during weight transfer.
4. **Pose changes are not driven by a continuous skeletal state.** Named timeline blocks switch arm/leg parameters at segment boundaries. Smooth easing is used for selected values, but not every joint parameter is interpolated across every transition, so some changes can read as pose swaps rather than connected motion.
5. **Opponent reactions are mostly hand-authored exceptions.** A few time ranges alter the second character's pose, but reactions are not derived from the other character's actual hand trajectory, contact point, or direction of movement.

### Medium priority — visual clarity and tests
6. **Effects are positioned at the midpoint between characters**, not at the detected contact location; large arcs can obscure the silhouettes.
7. **The renderer has no semantic choreography tests.** Current tests do not check bone-length stability, ground contact, minimum distance/contact at impact, character overlap, or frame-to-frame joint velocity.
8. **Existing tests are smoke tests only.** They establish that the script renders and that frames differ, but do not establish believable movement or reference similarity.

## Recommended implementation order
1. Establish one normalized joint-based rig representation per character (shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee, ankle, head, torso angle).
2. Use fixed bone lengths and solve elbow/knee positions from shoulder/wrist and hip/ankle targets; keep a consistent rig scale.
3. Add explicit choreography events with anticipation, action, contact, reaction, and recovery intervals. Trigger hit cues only when a hand target and opponent hit region are within a defined tolerance at the scheduled contact frame.
4. Add support-foot locks during planted phases and release them only at authored lift-off events; shift hips/torso over the support foot.
5. Drive the receiving character's recoil from contact point and movement direction, then blend back into a balanced stance.
6. Place impact graphics at the computed contact point and keep effects small enough not to hide the silhouettes.
7. Add automated checks for fixed limb lengths, planted-foot drift, contact proximity, frame-to-frame joint discontinuity, output dimensions, duration, and FFmpeg exit status.
8. Review a contact sheet and the complete MP4 at normal speed after each change. Keep reference videos as analysis inputs only; do not redistribute them in the repository.

## Acceptance criteria for the next revision
- Every impact effect coincides with visible contact or is explicitly labeled as a near-miss effect.
- Bone lengths remain stable across all frames (within rasterization tolerance).
- A planted foot moves no more than a small configured pixel tolerance before lift-off.
- The opponent's response begins after contact, follows a readable direction, and includes recovery.
- No limb crosses the torso unexpectedly; both hands remain connected to the arms.
- Unit tests pass, preview MP4 encodes successfully, and a human review confirms that the poses read clearly at normal playback speed.

## Current conclusion
The package is a working procedural prototype, not yet a reliable reusable animation system. The most important next change is to replace pose-coefficient limb drawing with a joint rig plus explicit contact/foot-lock constraints. Increasing visual effects or adding more timeline beats before those foundations are fixed would not solve the reported quality problems.
